Can a keyboard make you a more prolific writer?

We are about to find out.

______________________________________________

Keyboards. Keebs. It's a whole thing.

Yesterday my new Royal Kludge Keyboard arrived. I waited til after work hours to unbox it.  Surprisingly heavy. My first concern was how much weight it would add to my suitcase - because I knew at first sight that Kludgey and I would be inseparable from here on out.  It was white and split into two pieces, by design - Right hand, left hand. Big, chonky, heavy blocks.  I started calculating how much it was going to add to my checked-bag weight in my head.  

I sat on my bed with this weird set of tweezers and went through the tedious (and finger-stinging) process of pulling off every key cap, replacing every lego-like switch under the key, and putting each key back on.  Never having done such a weirdly geeky thing before I put my phone off to the side to play YouTube Videos about said geeky process just to make sure I wasn't going to fuck it all up.

As I sat there on my bed with hundreds of little lego-like keys around me, I was already starting to doubt myself. The doubts weren't the same that my husband was having. I wasn't asking myself "Am I right in the head?" instead it was headier questions like "Should I have gone with thocky or creamy?" "Shit - are these switches pre-lubed?" and "Should I be doing this at a table?" 

Luckily for me, the switches were in fact pre-lubed, aapparently most are these days. Were my switches lubed enough for some of these keyboard-afficiandos? Probably not. I decided it was not worth it to spend another 5 hours individually lubing each switch with a tiny paintbrush. That's a commitment I wasn't ready to make.

So I got all my switches in, keys back on.  Q W E R T Y.  A S D F G. My fingertipss were stinging but I was weirdly satisfied with myself. At the least I was learning something. There was a whole new "Keyboard World" out there and I was just stepping into it.  According to my new influencer friends on YouTube there might even be a "Keyboard Meetup" in my city where people meet to show off their keyboards and try new things.  I turned out the light, anticipating the next morning like I used to on Christmas Eve. The world was crazy out there. Grounded planes.  Banks shut down.  Meanwhile I was tucked into bed. A new RK-S70 loaded with fresh Jade KAILH 3 PIN Clickies on my nightstand.

Was it worth it? I can officially & emphatically say, as I write this very sentence with my new clicky Royal Kludge hotswappable keyboard, so worth it. 

How did I even get here?  Well, I guess the idea was hatched somewhere between watching CBS's Sunday Morning featuring the author of Pachinko Min Jin Lee and binge-watching I.V. Marie on Instagram.  On Sunday November 15th, the classic Sunday Morning show announced they were going to start recommending two books every week - one fiction and one non-fiction. Which that, in and of itself, is amazeballs. Because those books are going to be good. But I digress.

On the show that day they were featuring Min Jin Lee and at one point they panned across what appeared to be her writing setup - a standing desk, with a treadmill underneath, and an ergonomic black keyboard attached to her laptop. The keyboard (still unsure of the brand - if you know please type in the comments) seemed to leave a wide space between the right hand and the left hand.  I was intrigued.

30 years of working at a keyboard have brought me all kinds of aches and pains.  I'm not so much a "carpal tunnel" gal as an "over extended shoulder" gal - with one side of my body over-stretched and the other one tight as hell.  This causes a pain that radiates down the whole right side of my body and settles in the right buttock.  Lest I bore you any more with my particular brand of aches and pains, suffice it to say, it's a lot of cumulative years of repetitive strain I've put on neck, arms, back and shoulders. 

Something about Lee's writing setup looked like she wasn't destroying her body while she pushes out her doorstop-thick tomes. BTW, her new book, American Hagwon, made it to my TBR list.

Meanwhile over on Bookstagram I was also binge-watching I.V. Marie, a fanstasy writer who films her writing sessions. What I love about I.V. Marie, even though I haven't read her books, is that in addition to having an inspiring backstory, she has really nailed the promotion part of being a best-selling author - probably to her publishers delight. Honestly, she's a publisher's dream. She has a super clicky keyboard and she tags her posts ASDM, which I had no idea was an actual thing until I was this week years old. Her keyboard itself, although clicky, is all in one piece, but she has an admirabe clound wrist wrest.

Thanks to Min Jin and I.V. I fell into the the ergonomic keyboard/pomodoro-burst/ASDM rabbit hole that ended up with me at 10PM sitting in my bed surrounded by hundreds of little lego-looking switches with tweezers. I also blame those two for the algorithm gods offering me a post by "Team Creamy".  I see you over there Team Creamy and Team Thocky but the Harry Potter sorting hat has rightly thrown me in the House of Clickerin and this is where I'm staying.

So let me clue you in to why I'm super statsifed right now.

First of all, this keyboard breaks into two pieces. and you can separate the two pieces by about two feet. So this keeps your hands far apart.  Keeping your hands far apart while typing opens up your chest. Opening up your chest reduces the tendency to hunch. That in turn encourages you to sit straight up while typing.

This may seem like a small thing - typing with an open chest/spread arms versus hunchy/crunchy arms. But it's actually not. In yoga one of the most satisfying stretches for keyboard warriors is anything that falls in the "chest opener" category. It kind of undoes all the trauma that you put on your body.

The second great thing is that each side of the keyboard, the one under the right hand and left hand right now can be elevated to a customized degree which I'm sure helps with all the carpally-tunnelly people.

So now, you would think, how hard is it to actually get used to typing with your hands far apart? For me it was super easy. It took me less than 5 minutes to type at the same exact speed and ease. It was literally a five minute adjustment.

Next up, let's talk about what all those hotswappable little keys did for me. I decided to get a clicky set.  Now apparently you might judge me here. First of all, if you live with or have ever been annoyed by someone with a super loud keyboard - you can probably just stop reading this essay now.  Also, if you're part of the "thocky" or "creamy" or "silent" school, yeah apparently you're judging me too.

Oh, but my fellow clicky people. I see you. I hear you now.  Each key has a certain bounce and a nice satisfying click. Writing each sentence so enjoyable. I honestly could do this all day. All night. 

Click. Click. Clack. 

Divine...
